# Conceptual integrity

## Empirical results

For each displayed coefficient or moment, verify:

- Sign and units.
- Sample and weighting.
- Outcome definition.
- Shock normalization.
- Horizon and accumulation.
- Rounding.
- Whether uncertainty is displayed, omitted, or discussed.

Describe digitized estimates as digitized and avoid false precision.

## Dynamic worker problems

Make the worker's state and choice menu concrete before introducing value
functions. Distinguish current payoffs, continuation values, and realized
shocks. Do not call a dynamic choice probability a static employment share
unless the paper does.

## Sufficient statistics

Explain what each input does:

- Estimated flow responses show how behavior changes after the shock.
- Baseline choices reveal the relative welfare importance of adjustment
  margins.
- The first-order term weights the observed responses.
- The second-order term captures curvature and co-movement across margins.

Activate the same visual margins while introducing the corresponding term.
Full matrix notation is optional; the economic content is not.

## Welfare tables

State what is averaged, for whom, from which shock series, relative to which
baseline, over which dates and horizons, and with which discounting. Do not
present separately evaluated positive and negative paths as components of one
net transition.
