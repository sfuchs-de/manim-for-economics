# Paper brief

Complete this before writing animation code. Keep it to one or two pages.

## Audience

Who should understand the video, and what can they safely be assumed to know?

## One-sentence contribution

What does the paper establish that was not known before?

## Narrative format

Choose one format from `docs/visual-formats.md` and explain why it fits:

- general research explainer;
- mechanism-led;
- agent, choice, and welfare;
- empirical-result-led;
- method or theory.

Name the single research object that should persist across beats. It might be an
agent, network, market, treatment comparison, model object, theorem, or
measurement problem.

## Argument without notation

Describe the central logic without notation. Name the relevant agents, objects,
choices, constraints, comparisons, and mechanisms. Omit categories that the
paper does not use.

## Evidence

List the two or three empirical objects that carry the argument. For every
number, record its table, figure, or released-data source.

## Interpretation, welfare, or policy

What object is being measured or proved? Relative to what baseline or
counterfactual? Over what horizon? If the paper makes welfare or policy claims,
define them precisely. Otherwise state the relevant theoretical or empirical
interpretation.

## Visual vocabulary

Choose the visual theme independently from the narrative format. State whether
`midnight` or `ivory` better fits the intended venue and viewing context; do
not infer the theme from the paper's field.

Assign one stable color to each economically meaningful object. Prefer direct
labels over legends and small multiples over crowded composite charts.

State which pairs of representations must remain synchronized, such as a
network and a cost curve, treatment variation and an estimate, an agent's
choices and a welfare term, or a mathematical object and its geometric image.

## Claims to exclude

List tempting interpretations the design, data, or approximation does not
support.
