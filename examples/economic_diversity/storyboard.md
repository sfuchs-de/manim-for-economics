# Timed storyboard

| Beat | Learning goal | On-screen words | Visual action | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opening | Same shock, different menus | “Which city better protects workers?” | Two symmetric cities with different worker allocations | Mechanism |
| Worker adjustment | Name the margins | switch locally · move · leave employment | Highlight each route from the shocked cell | Dynamic worker problem |
| Diversity | Explain HHI economically | concentration summarizes the local menu | Compare alternatives after the same cell shock | HHI definition |
| Identification | Show the empirical shock | local exposure × national growth | Released city-year shocks as dots | Replication `data_bartik.dta` |
| Evidence | State asymmetric adjustment | higher HHI → larger declines after adverse shocks | Directly labeled IRFs | Published Figure 3 |
| Welfare | Build sufficient-statistic intuition | first-order response + second-order insurance | Group flows as stay, switch locally, and move; reveal the relevant routes with each term | Theory and Table 2 |
| Conclusion | State the contribution | diversity protects the downside | Return to two-city comparison | Supported synthesis |

The implementation intentionally omits the notation for the full Hessian and
value-recovery objects. The words-first decomposition is the middle ground
between no theory and the complete paper formula.
